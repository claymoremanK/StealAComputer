STEAL A COMPUTER! Game Concept — Updated Planning Draft Status: Early
development planning; core direction partially decided, many systems
still open

============================================================ 1. CORE
CONCEPT ============================================================

Steal A Computer! is a Roblox game about building computers using
real-world-inspired PC components that players choose and physically
assemble themselves.

Each player has a personal office in a city-themed world. The computers
they build and the hardware they collect are central to their progress,
income, hacking abilities, and cybersecurity defenses.

The intended experience combines: - Physical PC building and hardware
choices - Progression through earning and spending money - Automatic
crypto mining - Hacking, stealing, and defending against other players -
Strategic choices without making the game overly complicated

The current direction is to make computer hardware feel physical and
meaningful. Players should be able to carry GPUs, install them into
physical slots, expand their mining rigs, and improve their setup over
time.

============================================================ 2. WORLD
AND PLAYER OFFICES
============================================================

-   The game world has a city theme.
-   Each player has their own area designed to look like an office.
-   Players’ offices are arranged in a circle around the center of the
    map.
-   The central area contains:
    -   A computer-component store
    -   A bank
    -   Space for occasional NPC merchants to appear

The office is the player’s personal space for their computers, mining
equipment, and security setup.

The office is also the destination players must successfully return to
when carrying stolen GPUs if they want to keep those GPUs permanently.

============================================================ 3. BUYING
AND COLLECTING PC COMPONENTS
============================================================

Players buy components from a computer-component store in the center of
the city.

Stock system: - Some components are commonly available. - Rare,
powerful, or uncommon components appear less frequently and have limited
stock. - GPUs are intended to be especially rare compared with other
components.

An occasional NPC merchant may also appear in the central area and
offer: - Rare GPUs with limited stock - USB drives containing hacking
skills or abilities

GPU inventory and carrying: - Players have a limited capacity for
carrying GPUs. - The initial target is around 2–3 ordinary GPUs at a
time. - Some special GPUs may take up more inventory space. - Obtainable
backpacks may increase a player’s carrying capacity.

The exact inventory interface, backpack progression, and special GPU
capacity rules are still open for further design.

============================================================ 4. PHYSICAL
COMPUTER ASSEMBLY AND MINING RIGS
============================================================

Computer assembly is intended to be physical rather than entirely
interface-based.

GPU installation: 1. The player obtains a GPU. 2. The player physically
picks up and carries the GPU. 3. The player brings it to their computer
or mining rig. 4. The player manually positions and places it into an
available slot. 5. Once installed, the GPU contributes to the rig’s
mining output.

The intended direction is manual physical placement. Installation should
feel like placing an actual component into a computer, rather than
selecting a GPU from a menu.

Motherboards and capacity: - Each motherboard has a fixed number of GPU
slots. - Different motherboards may support different numbers of GPUs. -
Better motherboards can provide greater capacity.

Rig expansion: - Players can expand their mining setups using additional
physical expansion hardware, such as frames or expansion boards. - This
works alongside motherboard upgrades rather than replacing them. - The
exact expansion parts, limits, compatibility rules, and progression
costs have not yet been decided.

This creates two possible layers of progression: - Upgrade the
motherboard to gain more available slots. - Expand the overall mining
rig to accommodate a larger setup.

The exact assembly controls, component compatibility, and visual design
remain to be developed.

============================================================ 5. EARNING
MONEY: CRYPTO MINING
============================================================

Players earn in-game crypto by mining with installed GPUs.

Mining behavior: - Mining is automatic once GPUs are installed in a
mining rig. - Players do not need to repeatedly start mining sessions. -
Better or rarer GPUs generate more crypto. - Multiple installed GPUs
contribute to the player’s total mining output. - Larger mining setups
can increase income.

Offline mining: - Players can earn a limited amount of mining income
while away from the game. - Offline earnings are capped by a maximum
offline duration. - Four hours is an initial proposed cap, not a
finalized value. - The intended system is to calculate earnings from the
player’s mining setup and the time spent away, up to the cap.

Converting crypto into cash: 1. The player initiates the exchange on
their computer. 2. The exchanged amount becomes available to claim at
the bank in the center of the map.

This creates a choice for players: keep crypto available in their
computer or exchange it for cash. The idea is that unexchanged crypto
could be targeted by hackers.

The exact mining rates, offline calculation, economy balance, and
exchange rules remain undecided.

============================================================ 6. HACKING,
STEALING, AND OTHER PLAYERS
============================================================

Players can attempt to hack other players’ computers.

The original concept includes the possibility of a successful hack
allowing an attacker to: - Unlock another player’s office - Steal PC
components, especially GPUs - Steal the target player’s current,
unexchanged crypto balance

GPU stealing and escape: - A stolen GPU enters the thief’s carried
inventory. - The thief must successfully return to their own office to
keep the stolen GPU permanently. - If the thief dies or respawns before
successfully returning, the unbanked stolen GPU is lost and returns to
its victim’s inventory. - If the thief leaves the game before
successfully returning, the unbanked stolen GPU is lost and returns to
its victim’s inventory.

This makes successfully escaping with stolen hardware an important part
of the stealing loop.

Inventory limits: - Players initially carry around 2–3 GPUs at a time. -
Some special GPUs may take up more inventory space. - Obtainable
backpacks may increase carrying capacity.

Still undecided: - Whether installed GPUs can be stolen directly. -
Whether stealing installed GPUs requires overcoming office security. -
How hacking attempts begin and resolve. - How office access and locks
work. - Whether unexchanged crypto can be stolen and under what
conditions. - How victims recover or protect themselves. - How stolen
hardware is handled if other edge cases occur.

The stealing of installed GPUs has not yet been decided. Do not treat
direct removal, protected equipment, or conditional stealing as a
confirmed mechanic.

============================================================ 7.
CYBERSECURITY AND COMPUTER HARDWARE
============================================================

A player’s hacking and defense capabilities are influenced by the
components in their computer.

CPU: - Affects hacking speed and defense speed. - Influences overall
hacking capabilities and available defensive capabilities.

RAM: - RAM speed affects how quickly hacking actions can be performed. -
RAM capacity determines how many hacking or defensive features can be
handled at the same time. - RAM is envisioned as the computer’s active
working space or “inventory” during a hack or defense.

SSD: - SSD storage capacity determines how many hacking and defensive
features can be installed. - RAM capacity limits how many of those
installed features can be used at once. - This creates a strategic
choice: players must decide which features to install and which to use
together.

Other PC components may also influence hacking or defense, but their
specific roles have not yet been decided.

The relationship between the physical mining rig and the computer used
for hacking and defense still needs to be designed in detail.

============================================================ 8. HACKING
AND DEFENSE FEATURES
============================================================

-   Players can buy hacking and defensive features through a web
    interface on their in-game computer.
-   These features are purchased using in-game crypto.
-   Features have different strengths and weaknesses.
-   Players choose features based on their preferred hacking strategy
    and the target or situation they want to handle.
-   The goal is for hardware and feature selection to create strategy,
    rather than making the game depend only on who owns the most
    expensive components.

An occasional NPC hacker may sell USB drives containing hacking skills
or abilities.

The specific abilities, costs, limitations, and counterplay have not yet
been decided.

============================================================ 9. INTENDED
GAME FEEL ============================================================

The game should combine strategy with accessible progression.

-   It does not need to be extremely complex or overly strategic.
-   Players should be able to feel a steady sense of progression,
    similar to the satisfying progression found in Roblox games.
-   Hardware choices and hacking/defense features should add meaningful
    decisions without overwhelming players.
-   Physical assembly should make collecting and installing hardware
    feel satisfying.
-   Automatic mining should let players focus on building, upgrading,
    exploring, and competing rather than repeatedly managing mining
    sessions.

============================================================ 10.
CURRENTLY ENVISIONED PLAYER LOOP
============================================================

1.  Buy or obtain PC components.
2.  Physically carry GPUs to the computer or mining rig.
3.  Manually install GPUs into available slots.
4.  Upgrade the motherboard or expand the mining rig.
5.  Automatically generate crypto through mining.
6.  Accumulate limited offline earnings while away from the game.
7.  Exchange crypto for cash through the in-game computer.
8.  Claim the cash at the central bank.
9.  Spend money on better or rarer components.
10. Install hacking or defensive features using crypto.
11. Improve the office, mining setup, hacking capabilities, or defenses.
12. Attempt to hack or steal from other players, or defend against them.
13. If carrying stolen GPUs, successfully return to the office to keep
    them permanently.
14. Continue progressing through hardware upgrades and competition.

This is an updated interpretation of the current concept, not a
finalized gameplay sequence. The exact order and details remain open.

============================================================ 11. CURRENT
DECISIONS AND PROPOSED VALUES
============================================================

Confirmed planning decisions: - Physical, manual GPU placement. -
Motherboards have fixed GPU slot capacities. - Mining rigs can also be
expanded with additional hardware. - Mining is automatic while the
player is in the game. - Offline mining is limited rather than
unlimited. - Stolen GPUs enter the thief’s carried inventory. - A thief
must return to their office successfully to keep stolen GPUs. - Unbanked
stolen GPUs are lost on death/respawn or leaving the game, and return to
the victim’s inventory. - Initial carrying capacity is around 2–3
GPUs. - Special GPUs may take up more inventory space. - Backpacks can
be obtained to increase carrying capacity.

Proposed values that are not finalized: - Four-hour offline mining
cap. - Exact GPU slot counts for each motherboard. - Exact starting
inventory capacity and backpack upgrades. - Mining rates and hardware
prices.

These decisions describe the current planning direction. They are not
necessarily final implementation requirements.

============================================================ 13. CURRENT
STATUS AND NEXT STEP
============================================================

This document updates the initial concept with decisions made during
early planning. The game is still in the design stage. Many mechanics
remain open, and the concept should continue to be discussed with the
team.

The next step is to continue brainstorming the unresolved mechanics,
then define a small first playable prototype.

The first prototype should focus on a manageable slice of the core
experience rather than attempting to build every planned system at once.
