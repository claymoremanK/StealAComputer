STEAL A COMPUTER! Game Concept — Updated Planning Draft

Status: Early development planning. Core direction is partially decided.
The next phase is implementation planning and defining the first
playable prototype.

============================================================ 1. CORE
CONCEPT ============================================================

Steal A Computer! is a Roblox game about building computers using
real-world-inspired PC components, expanding GPU mining operations,
earning in-game crypto, and competing with other players through
hacking, stealing, and defense.

Each player has a personal office in a city-themed world. Their personal
PC supports hacking and cybersecurity, while separate GPU mining racks
generate crypto.

The intended experience combines: - Physical PC building and hardware
choices. - Physical GPU installation and removal. - Progression through
earning and spending money. - Automatic crypto mining. - Hacking,
stealing, and defending against other players. - Strategic choices
without making the game overly complicated.

The game’s main focus should remain computer building, mining, hacking,
stealing, and defense. Side activities should support this central
experience rather than turn the game into a generic job or errands
simulator.

============================================================ 2. WORLD
AND PLAYER OFFICES
============================================================

-   The game world has a city theme.
-   Each player has their own area designed to look like an office.
-   Players’ offices are arranged in a circle around the center of the
    map.
-   The central area contains a computer-component store, a bank, and
    space for occasional NPC merchants.

Each office is the player’s personal space for their personal PC, GPU
mining racks, and security setup. It is also the destination players
must successfully return to when carrying stolen GPUs if they want to
keep them permanently.

============================================================ 3. PERSONAL
PC AND GPU MINING RACKS
============================================================

The player’s setup consists of two separate systems.

A. Personal PC The personal PC is a physical computer assembled from PC
components. It handles the player’s hacking and cybersecurity
capabilities and provides access to in-game computer interfaces.

Current envisioned hardware roles: - CPU: Affects hacking speed and
defense speed. - RAM: RAM speed affects how quickly hacking actions can
be performed. RAM capacity determines how many hacking or defensive
features can be handled at the same time. - SSD: Storage capacity
determines how many hacking and defensive features can be installed. -
Motherboard: Supports the personal PC’s components. Exact compatibility
categories and specifications remain to be designed.

B. Independent GPU mining racks GPU mining racks are separate physical
structures in the player’s office. They operate independently from the
personal PC and generate crypto through installed GPUs.

Current direction: - Players start with one empty GPU rack. - Each rack
has a fixed number of GPU slots; exact capacity is undecided. - Players
physically carry GPUs to a rack and install them into available slots. -
Installed GPUs contribute to mining output. - Players expand their
mining operation by purchasing additional racks. - Each additional rack
is a separate physical structure. - Mining racks do not depend directly
on the personal PC’s motherboard for GPU capacity.

The personal PC and mining racks are separate gameplay systems within
the same office. Their detailed relationship, if any, remains open.

============================================================ 4. BUYING
AND COLLECTING PC COMPONENTS
============================================================

Players buy components from a computer-component store in the center of
the city.

Stock system: - Some components are commonly available. - Rare,
powerful, or uncommon components appear less frequently and have limited
stock. - GPUs are intended to be especially rare compared with other
components.

An occasional NPC merchant may also appear in the central area and offer
rare GPUs with limited stock and USB drives containing hacking skills or
abilities.

GPU inventory and carrying: - Players have limited capacity for carrying
GPUs. - Initial target: around 2–3 ordinary GPUs at a time. - Some
special GPUs may take up more inventory space. - Obtainable backpacks
may increase carrying capacity.

The exact inventory interface, backpack progression, and special GPU
capacity rules remain open.

============================================================ 5. PHYSICAL
COMPONENT ASSEMBLY
============================================================

Computer building is intended to be physical rather than entirely
interface-based.

Compatibility direction: - The game uses hybrid compatibility: building
should feel authentic and components should have meaningful differences,
but rules should be simplified compared with real-world PC building. -
Incompatible components cannot be installed. - The game should explain
why an incompatible component cannot be installed. - Exact compatibility
categories and requirements remain to be defined.

Personal PC assembly: - Players physically handle and install
components. - Exact placement controls and component compatibility rules
remain to be designed. - The personal PC’s motherboard supports its
compatible PC components.

GPU rack installation: 1. The player obtains a GPU. 2. The player
physically picks up and carries it. 3. The player brings it to an
available slot on a mining rack. 4. Available slots can be selected when
the player is close enough. 5. The GPU automatically aligns with the
selected slot (slot snapping). 6. The game checks whether the GPU can be
installed in that rack slot. 7. If installation is valid, the GPU
becomes installed and contributes to mining output. 8. If installation
is invalid, the game rejects it and explains why.

GPU removal: 1. The player approaches an installed GPU. 2. The player
interacts with it. 3. The GPU is detached from its slot. 4. The player
physically picks it up. 5. The player can carry it to another available
slot or otherwise use it.

The chosen direction is manual physical removal, not instant return to
an inventory menu.

Exact physical controls, rack appearance, slot layout, and edge cases
remain to be developed.

============================================================ 6. MINING
AND EARNING MONEY
============================================================

Players earn in-game crypto by mining with installed GPUs in their
mining racks.

Mining behavior: - Mining is automatic once GPUs are installed in a
mining rack. - Players do not need to repeatedly start mining
sessions. - Better or rarer GPUs generate more crypto. - Multiple
installed GPUs contribute to the player’s total mining output. -
Purchasing additional racks allows players to expand their mining
capacity.

Offline mining: - Players can earn a limited amount of mining income
while away from the game. - Offline earnings are capped by a maximum
offline duration. - Four hours is an initial proposed cap, not
finalized. - Earnings are intended to be calculated from the player’s
mining setup and time away, up to the cap.

Converting crypto into cash: 1. The player initiates the exchange on
their computer. 2. The exchanged amount becomes available to claim at
the bank in the center of the map.

Unexchanged crypto could be targeted by hackers. Exact mining rates,
offline calculation, economy balance, and exchange rules remain
undecided.

============================================================ 7. STARTING
EQUIPMENT AND EARLY-GAME PROGRESSION
============================================================

The chosen starting direction is a partially assembled setup.

When a player first joins, the intended starting equipment is: - A basic
personal office. - One already-assembled personal PC. - One empty GPU
mining rack. - No starting GPU. - Some starting cash.

The exact starting cash amount is undecided.

Players use their starting cash toward their first GPU purchase. If they
need more money, they can earn additional money through simple
computer-related tasks.

The chosen activity direction is computer tasks, not general city
errands or NPC computer repair jobs. Possible examples discussed
include: - File organization. - Basic troubleshooting. - Data
processing. - System optimization.

These are ideas, not finalized tasks. They should be simple supporting
activities that help players get started. The main gameplay should
remain mining, hacking, computer building, and competition.

Exact task designs, rewards, availability, and whether these activities
remain useful later are undecided.

============================================================ 8. HACKING,
STEALING, AND OTHER PLAYERS
============================================================

Players can attempt to hack other players’ computers.

The original concept includes the possibility of a successful hack
allowing an attacker to: - Unlock another player’s office. - Steal PC
components, especially GPUs. - Steal the target player’s current,
unexchanged crypto balance.

GPU stealing and escape: - A stolen GPU enters the thief’s carried
inventory. - The thief must successfully return to their own office to
keep the stolen GPU permanently. - If the thief dies or respawns before
successfully returning, the unbanked stolen GPU is lost and returns to
its victim’s inventory. - If the thief leaves the game before
successfully returning, the unbanked stolen GPU is lost and returns to
its victim’s inventory.

This makes successfully escaping with stolen hardware an important part
of the stealing loop.

Inventory limits: - Players initially carry around 2–3 GPUs at a time. -
Some special GPUs may take up more inventory space. - Obtainable
backpacks may increase carrying capacity.

Still undecided: - Whether installed GPUs can be stolen directly. -
Whether stealing installed GPUs requires overcoming office security. -
How hacking attempts begin and resolve. - How office access and locks
work. - Whether unexchanged crypto can be stolen and under what
conditions. - How victims recover or protect themselves. - How stolen
hardware is handled in other edge cases.

The stealing of installed GPUs has not yet been decided. Do not treat
direct removal, protected equipment, or conditional stealing as
confirmed mechanics.

============================================================ 9.
CYBERSECURITY AND HACKING FEATURES
============================================================

A player’s hacking and defense capabilities are influenced by components
in their personal PC.

Current envisioned hardware roles: - CPU affects hacking speed and
defense speed. - RAM speed affects how quickly hacking actions can be
performed. - RAM capacity determines how many hacking or defensive
features can be handled at the same time. RAM is envisioned as the
computer’s active working space during a hack or defense. - SSD storage
capacity determines how many hacking and defensive features can be
installed. - RAM capacity limits how many installed features can be used
at once.

Other PC components may also influence hacking or defense, but their
specific roles have not yet been decided.

Players can buy hacking and defensive features through a web interface
on their in-game computer: - Features are purchased using in-game
crypto. - Features have different strengths and weaknesses. - Players
choose features based on their preferred hacking strategy and the target
or situation. - Hardware and feature selection should create strategy,
rather than making the game depend only on who owns the most expensive
components.

An occasional NPC hacker may sell USB drives containing hacking skills
or abilities.

Specific abilities, costs, limitations, and counterplay have not yet
been decided.

Hacking progression remains unresolved. One proposed direction is to
make the hacking interface available early while requiring players to
obtain or install tools before performing meaningful hacking actions.
This has not been selected as a final decision.

============================================================ 10.
INTENDED GAME FEEL
============================================================

The game should combine strategy with accessible progression.

-   It does not need to be extremely complex or overly strategic.
-   Players should feel a steady sense of progression, similar to
    satisfying progression in Roblox games.
-   Hardware choices and hacking/defense features should add meaningful
    decisions without overwhelming players.
-   Physical assembly should make collecting and installing hardware
    feel satisfying.
-   Automatic mining should let players focus on building, upgrading,
    exploring, and competing rather than repeatedly managing mining
    sessions.
-   The central identity should remain mining, hacking, stealing, and
    defending—not unrelated side jobs.

============================================================ 11.
CURRENTLY ENVISIONED PLAYER LOOP
============================================================

1.  Join the game and receive a personal office.
2.  Start with an assembled personal PC, an empty GPU mining rack, and
    some starting cash.
3.  Visit the component store and inspect available GPUs.
4.  Complete simple computer-related tasks if additional money is
    needed.
5.  Purchase a first GPU.
6.  Physically carry the GPU back to the office.
7.  Install the GPU into an available mining rack slot using slot
    snapping.
8.  Automatically generate crypto through mining.
9.  Expand the mining operation by purchasing additional racks and GPUs.
10. Upgrade the personal PC to improve hacking and defensive
    capabilities.
11. Install hacking or defensive features using crypto.
12. Exchange crypto for cash through the in-game computer and claim it
    at the central bank.
13. Attempt to hack or steal from other players, or defend against them.
14. If carrying stolen GPUs, successfully return to the office to keep
    them.
15. Continue progressing through hardware upgrades, mining expansion,
    and competition.

This is an updated interpretation of the current concept, not a
finalized gameplay sequence. The exact order and details remain open.

============================================================ 12. CURRENT
DECISIONS AND PROPOSED VALUES
============================================================

Confirmed planning decisions: - The game centers on physical computer
building, GPU mining, hacking, stealing, and defense. - Each player has
a personal office in a city-themed world, arranged around a central
area. - The player’s setup has a personal PC and separate, independent
GPU mining racks. - The personal PC handles hacking and cybersecurity. -
GPU mining racks generate crypto independently from the personal PC. -
Players expand mining capacity by purchasing additional separate
racks. - The game uses hybrid component compatibility: authentic-feeling
but simplified. - Incompatible components cannot be installed. - GPU
installation uses slot snapping. - GPU removal is manual and physical. -
Mining is automatic while the player is in the game. - Offline mining is
limited rather than unlimited. - The starting setup is partially
assembled: assembled personal PC, one empty GPU rack, no GPU, and some
starting cash. - Players can earn additional money through simple
computer-related tasks. - Early tasks should support the main mining and
hacking gameplay, not replace it. - Stolen GPUs enter the thief’s
carried inventory. - A thief must return to their office successfully to
keep stolen GPUs. - Unbanked stolen GPUs are lost on death/respawn or
leaving the game, and return to the victim’s inventory. - Initial
carrying capacity is around 2–3 GPUs. - Special GPUs may take up more
inventory space. - Backpacks can be obtained to increase carrying
capacity.

Proposed values and details not finalized: - Four-hour offline mining
cap. - Exact starting cash and initial GPU price. - Exact GPU rack slot
capacity. - Exact compatibility categories and requirements. - Exact
computer-task designs and rewards. - Exact mining rates, hardware
prices, and economy balance. - Exact starting inventory capacity and
backpack upgrades. - Exact hacking progression and feature requirements.

============================================================ 14. CURRENT
STATUS AND NEXT STEP
============================================================

The concept has progressed from broad brainstorming into a more concrete
early design. The main direction is clearer, but many detailed mechanics
remain open.

The next step is to stop expanding the design indefinitely and begin
preparing for development.

The team should define a small first playable prototype focused on a
manageable slice of the core experience. It should demonstrate the
central computer/mining loop before attempting to implement every
planned system.

The prototype should be achievable for a beginner Roblox development
team. Hacking, advanced defenses, player stealing, offline earnings,
complex component compatibility, and the full economy can be introduced
in stages rather than all at once.

This document records the current planning direction. Any item
explicitly marked undecided or proposed must remain open until the team
makes a decision.
